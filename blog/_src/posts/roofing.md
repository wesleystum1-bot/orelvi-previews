Roofing is a trade where most customers are not shopping for fun. Something is leaking, a storm just came through, or an inspector flagged the roof during a home sale. They pick up a phone, search, and call within minutes. Your website has to win that moment.

## The first screen does the selling

Before anyone scrolls, the page should make four things obvious:

- **What you do:** repair, replacement, inspections, and the roof types you work on (shingle, tile, metal, flat).
- **Where you work:** the towns you cover.
- **How to reach you now:** a large tap-to-call button, and emergency availability if you offer it.
- **Why you are safe to hire:** your license number and insurance, if your state requires or you carry them.

If any of those are missing above the fold, a homeowner on a phone may move on to the next result.

## Name the jobs people actually search for

Homeowners search for the problem, not the trade. Give each common job its own short section with plain wording:

- Roof leak repair
- Storm and hail damage inspections
- Full roof replacement
- Tile or shingle repair
- Flat roof coating or repair
- Gutter work, if you do it

A sentence or two each is enough. Naming the job is what helps both the customer and Google match you to the search.

## Show your own roofs

Before-and-after photos of real jobs are the strongest proof a roofer has. Take them on your phone at every job: the damage, the work in progress, the finished roof from the street. A dozen of your own photos will outperform any stock image, and customers can tell the difference.

## Explain how the job works

Big-ticket jobs make people nervous. A short "How it works" section lowers that:

1. Call or send a request.
2. We inspect the roof and send photos of what we found.
3. You get a written quote.
4. We schedule the work and clean up when we're done.

Only describe the process you actually follow. If you work with insurance claims, say how you handle them, plainly and without promises about outcomes.

## Make the estimate request short

Name, phone, address or town, and what is going on. That's enough. Every extra field makes some people give up. You can ask the rest when you call back.

## Keep it fast and simple

Storm traffic comes in bursts, often on mobile data. Skip the autoplaying video and the slider. A single fast page with your services, area, photos and a call button will do more for you than a ten-page site that loads slowly.
