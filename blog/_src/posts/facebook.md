Plenty of good businesses run on a Facebook page, a Google Business Profile and word of mouth. If the phone is ringing, it is fair to ask why you would pay for a website at all.

The honest answer: those profiles do some jobs very well and some jobs not at all. Here is how to tell which side you are on.

## What a Google Business Profile does well

Your Google profile is free and it is often the first thing a local customer sees. It shows your hours, your reviews, your location and a call button right in the search results. **If you do not have one, set it up before anything else.** No website replaces it.

## What a Facebook page does well

Facebook is good for posting recent work, staying in touch with past customers and getting recommendations in local groups. For some trades, it is where referrals happen.

## Where they fall short

- **You do not control them.** The layout, the ads next to your posts and the rules can change at any time. Accounts get locked and pages lose reach.
- **They look like everybody else.** Every profile has the same layout. Nothing about it tells a customer why you are better than the business above you.
- **Not everyone uses Facebook.** A customer without an account hits a login wall when they try to look at your page.
- **They do not answer the real questions.** Which services do you offer? Which towns do you cover? What does a typical job involve? A website lets you answer all of that once, in your own words.
- **They are weaker in search for what you do.** A profile helps people who search your name. A website helps people who search for the service, such as "roof repair near me" or "mobile detailing in Mesa", find you in the first place.

## Credibility is the quiet reason

Many customers check for a website before they call, especially for larger jobs. A business with only a Facebook page can look like a side hustle, even when it is not. A clean, fast site that lists your services and area tells people you are established.

## When a website starts paying for itself

You are probably ready for a website if any of these are true:

- You want jobs from people who do not already know your name.
- You do larger jobs where customers compare a few businesses before calling.
- You get asked the same questions on every call.
- You want to look as established as the competitor who keeps winning the bigger jobs.
