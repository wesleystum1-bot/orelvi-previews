Most people do not enjoy choosing a mechanic. The check engine light is on, the brakes are grinding, or the car will not start, and they are worried about being overcharged. They search on a phone, look at two or three shops, and call or drive to the one that feels most honest. Your website is often where that decision is made.

## Make the basics impossible to miss

Before anyone scrolls, the top of the page should show:

- **What you work on:** the services you offer and any makes you specialize in or do not take.
- **Where you are:** your address with a link that opens in their maps app.
- **When you are open:** your real hours, kept up to date.
- **How to reach you:** a tap-to-call button, big enough for a thumb.

A driver standing next to a broken-down car will not hunt for any of these.

## List the jobs people search for

People search for the problem or the job. A short line for each service you offer helps them and helps Google match you to those searches:

- Check engine light diagnostics
- Brakes
- Oil changes and scheduled maintenance
- AC repair
- Suspension, steering and alignment
- Engine and transmission work
- Inspections or emissions testing, if your state requires them and you do them

Only list what you actually do. Someone who calls for a job you turn away will remember it.

## Earn trust before they arrive

Trust decides most repair-shop choices. Show the things that build it:

- Certifications your technicians actually hold, such as ASE, if they do.
- Any warranty you give on parts and labor, stated exactly as you honor it.
- How you handle estimates: for example, that you call with the price before doing work beyond what was approved, if that is how you work.
- Your Google reviews, linked, or quoted word for word with the name the customer posted under.
- Real photos of your bays, your team and your shop sign, so people recognize the place when they pull in.

## Let people book or drop off easily

If you take appointments, make booking simple: a short form with name, phone, vehicle, and what is going on, or a link to the booking tool you already use. If you take walk-ins or have a key drop, say so and explain how it works.

## Say what to expect while they wait

A line or two on what happens after drop-off removes worry. Do you offer a waiting area, a shuttle, or loaner cars? Do you text updates? Only describe what you actually provide.

## Keep it fast on a phone

Many visitors are on mobile data, sometimes at the side of the road. Skip autoplaying video and sliders. A single fast page with your services, hours, address, reviews and call button does more than a big site that loads slowly.
