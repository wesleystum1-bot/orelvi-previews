Most plumbing customers find you in a hurry. Water is on the floor, the water heater quit, or the drain will not clear. They search on a phone, glance at a few results, and call. Your website does not need to be big. It needs to make that call easy and make you look like the safe choice.

## Put the call button where a thumb can reach it

The phone number should be a real tap-to-call link, large, near the top, and repeated at the bottom. A number shown only as text, or tucked inside a picture, means the customer has to copy it by hand. Many will not bother.

If you take emergency calls, say so in plain words next to the button, along with the hours you actually answer. If you do not, say when you do answer, so nobody calls at midnight and gets angry at voicemail.

## Say where you work

List the towns or areas you cover by name. People want to know before they call whether you will come to them, and naming the towns helps Google show you to people searching in them.

## Name the jobs people search for

Customers search for the problem, not for "plumbing services." A short line for each job you take helps both the customer and the search engine:

- Leak repair and burst pipes
- Drain cleaning and clogs
- Water heater repair and replacement
- Toilet, faucet and fixture repair
- Sewer line problems
- Water softeners or filtration, if you install them

Only list what you really do. A customer who calls for a job you turn down is a lost call for both of you.

## Show you are safe to let in the house

A plumber walks into someone's home. Show the things that make that feel safe:

- Your license number, if your state licenses plumbers.
- That you are insured, if you are.
- Real photos of you, your truck and your work, not stock images.
- Your Google reviews, linked or quoted word for word with the customer's name as they posted it.

## Be clear about how pricing works

You do not have to publish prices. But a sentence on how you charge, such as a trip fee, a free estimate, or a quote before any work starts, removes a worry that stops some people from calling. Describe only what you actually do.

## Keep the form short

Name, phone, town and what is going on. That is enough to call back. Every extra box costs you some requests, especially on a phone.

## Make it fast on a phone

Someone with water on the floor will not wait for a slow page. Skip the autoplaying video and the sliding banners. One quick page with your call button, your area, your services and your photos does more than a large site that crawls on mobile data.
