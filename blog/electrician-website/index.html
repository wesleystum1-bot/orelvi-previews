What an electrician's website needs to get the call

People hire an electrician for safety, not just price. Here is what your website has to show so a homeowner or business owner calls you instead of the next result.

Electrical work is a trade where customers are nervous before they call. A breaker keeps tripping, an outlet is warm, the lights flicker, or they need a panel upgrade to add an EV charger and have no idea what it costs. They search on a phone, look at a few sites, and call the one that seems most trustworthy. Your website's job is to be that one.

Put the call button at the top

Your phone number should be a real tap-to-call link, large, near the top of the page, and repeated at the bottom. A number written as plain text or built into a picture forces people to copy it by hand, and some will not.

If you take emergency calls, say so next to the button with the hours you actually answer. If you do not, give your real hours so nobody expects a call back at 2 a.m.

Lead with safety and credentials

Electrical work is licensed in most states, and customers know a bad job can be dangerous. Make the things that prove you are safe to hire easy to find:

  • Your license number, if your state licenses electricians.
  • That you are insured, if you are.
  • Whether you pull permits for the work that needs them.
  • Real photos of you, your van and your finished work, not stock images.

Name the jobs people search for

Customers search for the job or the problem. A short line for each type of work you take helps them, and helps Google match you to those searches:

  • Troubleshooting and repairs (tripping breakers, dead outlets, flickering lights)
  • Panel upgrades and replacements
  • EV charger installation
  • Lighting, ceiling fans and outdoor lighting
  • Outlets, switches and GFCI protection
  • Generator hookups or whole-home surge protection, if you do them
  • Commercial or new construction work, if you take it

Only list what you do. A call for work you turn away costs you both time.

Split homeowners and businesses if you serve both

A homeowner with a flickering light and a property manager with a tenant build-out want different things. If you serve both, give each its own short section so each one quickly sees you handle their kind of job.

Say where you work

List the towns or areas you cover by name. People check this before they call, and naming the towns helps you show up when someone searches in them.

Explain how a job goes

A few lines lower the worry of letting an electrician in:

  1. Call or send a request with what is going on.
  2. We come out, find the problem and explain it in plain words.
  3. You get the price before any work starts.
  4. We do the work, test it, and clean up.

Describe only the process you really follow.

Keep the form short and the page fast

Name, phone, town and what is going on is enough to call back. Every extra box loses some requests on a phone. And keep the page light: skip autoplaying video and sliders, so it opens quickly on mobile data.
