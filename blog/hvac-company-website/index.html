What an HVAC company website needs when the AC quits in July

When the AC dies or the furnace will not light, people search on a phone and call the first company that looks ready to come out. Here is what your website has to show them.

Heating and cooling work comes in two kinds. There is the urgent call, when the AC quits on a hot afternoon or the heat goes out on a cold night, and the planned job, like a tune-up, a new system or a maintenance plan. Your website has to handle both, and the urgent call comes first, because that customer will call whoever looks ready soonest.

Make calling one tap

Put your phone number near the top as a real tap-to-call link, big enough to hit with a thumb, and repeat it at the bottom of the page. A number that is plain text, or part of a picture, makes a hot, frustrated customer copy it by hand. Many will go back to the search results instead.

Next to the button, say when you answer. If you offer same-day or after-hours service, say so in plain words. If you do not, say your real hours, so nobody expects a call back at midnight.

Say which areas you serve

Name the towns or neighborhoods you cover. People want to know you will come to them before they call, and listing the towns by name helps Google show you to people searching there.

List the jobs people search for

Customers search for the problem, not for "HVAC services". Give each kind of job you take a short line or two:

  • AC repair and AC not cooling
  • Furnace or heat pump repair
  • New AC or heating system installs and replacements
  • Seasonal tune-ups and maintenance plans
  • Ductwork, thermostats or indoor air quality, if you do them

Only list what you actually do. A call for a job you turn down wastes your time and theirs.

Explain repair or replace

The most stressful question for a homeowner is whether to fix the old unit or buy a new one. A short section on how you help them decide builds trust before they call: you look at the unit, explain what you found, and give a written quote for each option. Describe only the process you actually follow, and do not promise savings or outcomes you cannot back up.

If you sell maintenance plans, show what is in them

A maintenance plan is steady work for you and fewer surprise breakdowns for the customer. If you offer one, say what it includes, how often you visit and what it costs, or that you will quote it. A plan nobody can find on your site is a plan nobody signs up for.

Show you are safe to let in

You are coming into someone's home, often while they are uncomfortable and in a hurry. Show what makes that feel safe:

  • Your license number, if your state licenses HVAC contractors.
  • That you are insured, if you are.
  • Real photos of your team, your vans and your installs instead of stock images.
  • Your Google reviews, linked, or quoted word for word with the name the customer posted under.

Keep the request form short

Name, phone, town and what is going on. That is enough to call them back. Each extra box loses some requests, especially from someone typing on a phone in a hot house.

Make it fast on a phone

Someone whose AC just stopped is not going to wait for a slow page. Skip autoplaying video and sliding banners. One quick page with your call button, your area, your services and real photos does more than a big site that loads slowly on mobile data.
