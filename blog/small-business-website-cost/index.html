What a small business website costs in 2026, and what you are actually paying for

Website quotes range from free to five figures. Here is what sits behind each price, which costs repeat every year, and how to compare quotes without getting burned.

Ask five people what a website costs and you will get five answers between "free" and "fifteen thousand dollars". They are all telling the truth. The price depends on who builds it, how much of the work you do yourself, and what you keep paying for after launch.

This guide breaks the cost into the parts that actually move the number, so you can read any quote and know what you are buying.

The four ways a business website gets made

1. You build it yourself on a website builder

Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy and similar builders charge a monthly plan, usually somewhere in the range of $15 to $50 a month for a business plan. The software is cheap. Your time is not. Most owners who go this route spend a few weekends on it, and the result looks like a template because it is one.

Good fit if: you enjoy this kind of work and have the time. Watch out for: the plan price going up at renewal, and the site being hard to move if you ever leave the builder.

2. A freelancer

Freelance quotes for a small business site commonly land anywhere from a few hundred dollars to a few thousand. The quality range is just as wide. The best freelancers are excellent; the risk is paying a deposit and waiting weeks for a first draft you do not like.

3. A traditional agency

Agencies bring a team, a process and a portfolio, and price accordingly. Expect several thousand dollars and up, often with a discovery phase, a deposit, and a timeline measured in weeks or months. For a large company that needs custom features, that can be money well spent. For a roofer who needs to show up on a phone and get calls, it is usually more than the job needs.

4. A productized studio

What you are really paying for

Whoever builds it, a website quote is made of the same ingredients:

  • Design. How the site looks and whether it was made for your trade or poured into a generic template.
  • Writing. Someone has to write the words. A site with vague copy loses calls even if it looks good.
  • Build and testing. Making it work on every screen size, especially phones, where most local customers will see it first.
  • Launch. Connecting your domain, setting up the contact form, and making sure Google can read the page.
  • Changes. How many rounds of edits are included before extra charges start.

When two quotes differ by thousands of dollars, the difference is almost always in how much of each ingredient you get, and how much of it is done by hand.

The costs that repeat every year

With most builders the build is a one-time cost and these come on top (with ours, the monthly plan covers hosting and changes):

  • Domain name. Usually around $10 to $20 a year for a .com.
  • Hosting. Anywhere from free (for a fast static site) to $30 a month or more on some platforms.
  • Builder subscription. If your site lives on a builder, you pay the plan for as long as the site exists.
  • Care or maintenance. Optional, but someone has to update hours, prices and services when they change. With ORELVI it is part of the monthly plan, not an extra.

Ask every quote: after launch, what do I pay each year, and what happens if I stop paying?

Five questions to ask before you pay anyone

  1. Can I see a real draft of my site before I pay a deposit?
  2. Who owns the site and the domain when we are done?
  3. How many rounds of changes are included?
  4. What does it cost per year after launch?
  5. Will it work properly on a phone? Can I check it on mine before it goes live?

If a quote cannot answer the first question with a yes, you are paying for a promise rather than a website.

What we would spend, if it were our business

For most local service businesses, a clean one-page site that loads fast on a phone, lists your services, area and hours, and makes calling you a single tap is enough to win the job. You do not need to spend five figures to get that. You do need it to be done properly, because a slow or broken site costs you customers you never hear about.
