How local customers actually find a business online

Your Google profile, your website and your reviews do different jobs. Here is how they work together when someone nearby searches for what you do, and what to set up first.

When someone nearby needs what you do, they usually take one of three paths: they search the service on Google, they open Google Maps, or they ask for a recommendation and then look you up. Each path leans on a different part of your online presence.

Path 1: "roof repair near me"

A search for a service plus a place, or "near me", typically shows a map with a handful of local businesses, then regular search results below it.

  • The map results come mostly from Google Business Profiles. Being listed in the right category, with accurate hours, a service area and genuine reviews, matters most here.
  • The regular results come from websites. A page that clearly names the service and the towns you cover gives Google something to match the search against. A profile alone cannot rank there.

Path 2: searching your name

Someone heard about you and searches your business name. Now they are checking you out. They will look at your reviews, your photos and, very often, your website. This is where a missing, broken or outdated website loses a customer who was already sold.

Path 3: a recommendation

A neighbour recommends you in a local group. The person still looks you up before calling. Same as path 2: your profile and website are the interview.

What to set up, in order

  1. Google Business Profile. Free. Correct category, hours, service area, phone and photos of real work. Ask happy customers for reviews.
  2. A fast website that names your services and area. One page is enough for many local businesses. Make the phone number tappable and put it at the top.
  3. Consistent details everywhere. Your name, phone number and address should match exactly across your profile, website, Facebook and directories. Inconsistent details confuse customers and search engines alike.
  4. Link them together. Your Google profile should link to your website, and your website should link to your reviews.

What actually moves the needle

  • Reviews from real customers, answered by you.
  • Clear pages that say what you do and where, in the words customers search with.
  • A site that works on phones, loads fast and makes calling easy.

There is no trick to it. Search engines are trying to send people to the business most likely to solve their problem. Make it obvious that it is you.
