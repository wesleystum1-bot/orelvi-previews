Order of work

How a job runs

  1. Phase 01

    Site visit and layout

    We walk the grades, mark where water goes now, and lay the shape out with paint so you can stand on it before anything is dug.

  2. Phase 02

    Written proposal

    Material named by manufacturer and colour, base depth stated in inches, square footage measured rather than estimated.

  3. Phase 03

    Excavation and base

    Utilities marked through PA One Call, soil hauled off, geotextile down, then base placed and plate-compacted in lifts of a few inches.

  4. Phase 04

    Setting and finishing

    Pavers or stone set to the layout, cuts made on a wet saw, edge restraint spiked, joints swept, compacted and locked.

  5. Phase 05

    Clean-up and walk-through

    Beds restored, site swept, and a walk with you covering settling, efflorescence and when to seal if you want to.

Notes

Asked before the first visit

01How deep does the base need to be?

Six inches of compacted 2A modified under a patio, ten to twelve under anything a vehicle drives on. In Bucks County clay we often go deeper, and the proposal says which.

02Pavers or bluestone?

Pavers are interlocking, forgiving of frost movement and easy to lift and re-set. Bluestone is irregular, looks right on older stone houses here, and costs more to cut and set. Both last if the base is right.

03What is the white haze on my pavers?

Efflorescence — salts moving out of the concrete as it cures. It usually weathers off within a year. A cleaner made for it will take it off sooner; pressure washing will not.

04Does a retaining wall need a permit?

In most of these townships, over four feet does, and over that height it needs an engineered design with geogrid. We file the drawings; you will see the engineer’s fee as its own line.

05Can you work in winter?

Excavation and base work, yes, until the ground is frozen. We do not set polymeric sand or mortar below about fifty degrees, so those steps wait for a warm stretch.
